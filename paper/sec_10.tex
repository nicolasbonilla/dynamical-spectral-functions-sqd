\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a spectral function reconstructed by Rayleigh--Ritz on a sampled subspace, the captured Born
weight does not bound the error; the weight the subspace missed, together with the Hamiltonian
coupling across its boundary, does. Theorem~\ref{thm:leakage}, for any orthogonal projector and
given the exact ground state, bounds the $L_1$ error from below by the Born weight the subspace
failed to capture and from above by that weight plus the coupling that leaks across the subspace
boundary at the resolution asked for, a quantity computed from the Ritz pairs and the retained part
of the probe, without the spectral function it bounds. The captured weight alone cannot replace it:
an analytic counterexample and a counting argument rule out any bound indexed on it, and on the
probe's own Born support, where the captured weight is exactly one, the relative error is still
$0.43$ at $L=6$ and $0.35$ at $L=8$ at $\eta=0.18\,t$, because the weight a subspace keeps is
displaced in $\omega$, not discarded (Sec.~\ref{sec:retraction}). What a Krylov-adequate subspace
does guarantee is the classical moment exactness of Krylov projections, through order $2K+1$ and no
further, a reach stated exactly and measured on coordinate subspaces (Theorem~\ref{thm:moments}).
And no orbital-rotation invariant bounds the boundary \emph{usefully} on every state in advance, on pairing-model fibers every such bound
being vacuous: one-body fermionic
magic is constant along an orbit that contains a point of classical cost $O(K)$, at bond dimension
$2$ and $2^{K}$ determinants in the pairing basis, and points at which sampling is plausibly hard, a
hardness not established here, and on fibers on which the subspace-aware certificate still separates
(Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}, Proposition~\ref{prop:leakinv}), consistent with the separation drawn by
Tarabunga \emph{et al.}~\cite{tarabunga2026} between covariance-matrix quantities, two of which
lower-bound the non-Gaussian gate count, and full-state natural-orbital participation entropies, which
upper-bound the classical simulation cost in an orthonormal Gaussian basis.

Evaluated on the subspaces of the resource scan at five system sizes up to a sector of dimension
$10\,306\,296$, the certificate is vacuous at every operating fraction of the resource scan, by
factors of $1.66$ to $8.61$, converged in Krylov depth at $L=12$, and informative only well above
them. Where it is informative it is calibrated: over sixteen $(L,\eta)$ cells at $L=6$--$12$ the
crossing of the trivial bound occurs at a true relative $L_1$ error between $1.2\times10^{-3}$ and
$7.3\times10^{-3}$, a drift, not scatter, ordered in both arguments and conservative in $L$,
computed without the spectral function it bounds, given the exact probe. A two-constant rule in
$1-w_S$, fitted out of sample, localizes the same crossing more tightly on eleven of the fourteen
splits; what distinguishes the certificate from such a rule is that it is an inequality with a
proof, whose decision survives replacing $w_S$ by a floor. The Born-ranked, infinite-shot
reconstruction of Fig.~\ref{fig:akwsampled}, at $85\%$ of its sector, is non-vacuous in all sixteen
channels, at a relative bound of $0.77$--$1.75$ against the trivial $2$, $350$--$900$ times its true
error. The basis dependence of the boundary term is measured as well. In the determinant basis a
device measures in, the coordinate support of the Krylov space $\mathcal K(H,\phi)$ is every
determinant the reflection about the seed site allows, measured at $L=6$ to $14$, so at full
captured weight a subspace holding the whole probe leaks unless it contains all of them; the vacuity
at the operating fractions, all below full captured weight, is measured and not deduced from that
fact. A momentum-resolved probe measured in plane-wave orbitals has its Krylov space inside one
momentum block instead, $1/L$ of the sector, on which the certificate still needs $455$--$483$ of
$490$ determinants at $L=8$, a gain of $1/L$ against an exponential
(Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier:obstruction}). The method is priced in support, resolution and shots
(Sec.~\ref{sec:prices}): the determinant support that reconstructs $A(\omega)$ to relative
$L_1<0.05$ at $\eta=0.15\,t$ grows as $e^{1.05\,L}$ over $L=4$--$14$, and the shot budget grows
from $7.8\times10^{2}$ to $1.9\times10^{7}$ over $L=4$--$12$. The full-sector references reproduce
the opposite finite-size scaling of the charge and spin gaps, the spin gap closing as $\approx2/L$
with an independent effective-Heisenberg control (Table~\ref{tab:gaps}).

The certificate does not certify the reconstructions at the operating fractions of the resource
scan, and the regime where it would costs more shots than the method saves: moving from the
operating fraction to the non-vacuous band multiplies the shots per snapshot needed for the marginal
configuration by $7.5$ to $10$ at $L=8$ and by $20$ to $35$ at $L=12$, and at the acceptance
criterion this work uses elsewhere, relative $L_1<0.05$, the certificate needs at least $0.98$ of
the sector at $L=8$, a fraction at which diagonalizing the whole sector is what one would do
instead, and one not reached on the grid at $L=6$, $10$ or $12$. The bound refers both spectra to
the exact ground-state energy (H1); in a shot-based pipeline $E_0$ is itself estimated on a sampled
subspace, and the bound then holds only after an additive term the certificate does not control; for the shift
of $6.7\times10^{-4}\,t$ that $60\%$ of the $N$ sector gives at $L=8$ that term moves the bound by only
$+0.20\%$, while the measured error grows by a factor $16$
(Sec.~\ref{app:hyp}). It builds the probe from the exact ground state (H4); from an approximate one
it bounds the error of that approximate probe's own spectral function, whose distance to the exact
one nothing computed here controls, and no certificate in this work has been evaluated with an
approximate probe. All spectra here are at exact-diagonalization sizes, the largest
momentum-resolved one at $L=12$, whereas dynamical DMRG computed the same three channels at $60$ to
$120$ sites in 2007~\cite{benthien2007} and correction vectors reach $L=48$ in the Hubbard
chain~\cite{nocera2016}; one dimension
is where tensor networks are near-optimal~\cite{whitedmrg1992,schollwock2011}, and the largest
minimal bond dimension over the $74$ exact ground states deposited here is $42$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:honest}).

Whether a certified regime exists at low sector fraction is now a well-posed measurement, and the
calibrated crossing is the instrument for making it. Whether it exists at all turns on two open
questions: whether the certificate of second order in the boundary coupling that follows from the
same identity at full captured weight has a counterpart below it, where the operating fractions
lie, that can be evaluated without the answer; and whether some single-particle basis gives
$\mathcal K(H,\phi)$ a coordinate support that is a small fraction of the sector, the plane-wave
block being one such basis for a momentum probe, at about $L(L-1)$ further two-qubit gates whose
effect on the sampled distribution is unmeasured, and with the filling of the block at $L\ge10$
unmeasured as well. What this work provides toward both is a two-sided bound whose boundary-leakage
term is computable from the Ritz pairs and the retained probe, a measured map of where it is
informative and at what cost, and the coordinate support that fixes, basis by basis, what zero
leakage at full captured weight requires. For a sampled pipeline validated at exact-diagonalization
scale, where the exact ground state is in hand, the consequence is that the captured weight is not a
convergence diagnostic for the spectral function built on the subspace; what bounds that error, as
an exclusion rather than an error estimate, is the weight the subspace missed together with the
boundary coupling, computable there from the same Ritz pairs, the retained probe and one
matrix--vector product per Ritz vector.
