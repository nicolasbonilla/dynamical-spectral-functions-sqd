\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a spectral function reconstructed by Rayleigh--Ritz on any subspace, Theorem~\ref{thm:leakage}
bounds the $L_1$ error from below by the Born weight the subspace failed to capture and from above by
that weight plus the Hamiltonian coupling that leaks across the subspace boundary at the resolution asked
for, a quantity computed from the Ritz pairs without the answer. The captured weight alone cannot
replace it: an analytic counterexample and a counting argument rule out any bound indexed on it
(Sec.~\ref{sec:retraction}). What a Krylov-adequate subspace does guarantee is moment exactness through
order $2K+1$ and no further (Theorem~\ref{thm:moments}). And no orbital-rotation invariant bounds the
boundary \emph{usefully} in advance: one-body fermionic magic is constant along an orbit on which the
determinant support runs from $O(K)$ to $2^{K}$ at bond dimension $2$, and on fibers on which the
subspace-aware certificate still separates (Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}, Proposition~\ref{prop:leakinv}),
the general invariant/basis-dependent division being due to Tarabunga \emph{et al.}~\cite{tarabunga2026}.

Evaluated at five system sizes up to a sector of dimension $10\,306\,296$, the certificate is vacuous at
every operating fraction of the resource scan, by factors of $1.66$ to $8.61$, converged in Krylov depth
at $L=12$; only Fig.~\ref{fig:akwsampled}, at $85\%$ of its sector, is certified, at $350$--$900$ times
its true error. Two results survive that measurement. The crossing of the trivial bound occurs at a true
relative $L_1$ error between $1.2\times10^{-3}$ and $7.3\times10^{-3}$ over sixteen $(L,\eta)$ cells, a
drift, not scatter, ordered in both arguments and conservative in $L$, computed without the answer---with the limit
that a two-constant rule in $1-w_S$, fitted out of sample, localizes it more tightly on eleven of the
fourteen splits, so we claim no superiority for the certificate as a tracker of the error against the
captured weight. And in the determinant basis a device measures in, the coordinate support of the Krylov
space $\mathcal K(H,\phi)$ is every determinant the reflection about the seed site allows, measured at
$L=6$ to $14$, so at full captured weight a subspace holding the whole probe leaks unless it contains all
of them (Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier:obstruction}); the vacuity at the operating fractions, all below full
captured weight, is measured and not deduced from that fact. The method is priced in support, resolution
and shots (Sec.~\ref{sec:prices}), and the full-sector references reproduce the opposite finite-size
scaling of the charge and spin gaps, the spin gap closing as $\approx2/L$ with an independent
effective-Heisenberg control (Table~\ref{tab:gaps}).

Three limits apply. \emph{First,} the certificate does not certify the reconstructions at the operating
fractions of the resource scan: at full captured weight a certificate of second order in the boundary
coupling follows from the same identity, but below it, where those fractions lie, one is open. The
certificate also refers both spectra to the exact ground-state energy (H1); in a shot-based pipeline
$E_0$ is itself estimated on a sampled subspace, and the shift of $6.7\times10^{-4}\,t$ that $60\%$ of
the $N$ sector gives at $L=8$ leaves the bound almost unchanged, $+0.20\%$, while multiplying the
measured error by $16$ (Sec.~\ref{sm:leakage}). \emph{Second,} the largest momentum-resolved spectrum
here is $L=12$, by exact diagonalization, whereas dynamical DMRG computed the same three channels at $90$
to $120$ sites in 2007~\cite{benthien2007}, correction vectors reach $L=48$ in the Hubbard
chain~\cite{nocera2016}, and the classical toolbox is broader
still~\cite{kuehner1999,whitefeiguin2004,holzner2011,kpm2006,paeckel2019,tdasci2024}; one dimension is
where tensor networks are near-optimal~\cite{whitedmrg1992,schollwock2011}, and nothing in this work
competes with that standard. \emph{Third,} the certified regime costs more shots than the method saves:
moving from the operating fraction to the non-vacuous band multiplies the shots per snapshot needed for
the marginal configuration by $7.5$ to $10$ at $L=8$ and by $20$ to $35$ at $L=12$, where diagonalizing
the whole sector is already the cheaper option.

Whether a certified regime exists at low sector fraction is now a well-posed measurement, and the
calibrated crossing is the instrument for making it. Whether it exists at all turns on two open
questions: whether a certificate of second order in the boundary coupling can be evaluated below full
captured weight without the answer, and whether some single-particle basis gives $\mathcal K(H,\phi)$ a
coordinate support that is a small fraction of the sector. What this work provides toward both is a
computable two-sided bound, a measured map of where it is informative and at what cost, and the
structural reason it is not informative at the fractions sampling reaches.
