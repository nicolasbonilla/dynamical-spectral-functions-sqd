\section{The operational question}
\label{sec:intro}

A sample-based quantum calculation returns a list of bitstrings. Diagonalizing the Hamiltonian
inside the span of the configurations they name yields an energy, and---as this work and, now,
several others show---a frequency-resolved response as well. What it does not yield is any
statement about how wrong that response is. This paper is built around the question a user is left
with once the shots are spent---\emph{given the subspace those shots populated, how wrong is the
spectral function built on it?}---and its principal result is an obstruction to answering it at the
fractions sampling reaches. A certificate of leakage type is non-vacuous only where the Hamiltonian
coupling across the boundary of the subspace is small, so we measured that boundary. The coordinate
support of the Krylov space $\mathcal K(H,\phi)$ is $296$ of $300$ determinants at $L=6$ and all
$3920$ of $3920$ at $L=8$; it is measured at $L=8$, $10$, $12$ and $14$, and at each it is the
whole sector at the declared threshold, by two estimators that agree
wherever both ran. From $L=8$ on the leakage is therefore strictly positive for \emph{every} proper
coordinate subspace, and no leakage-type certificate in that basis---ours or another's---can be
non-vacuous there at low sector fraction (Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier:obstruction}). That is a property
of the basis in which the device measures rather than of one inequality, and it is therefore
indexed on that basis rather than absolute: the one route out has been measured and it is
reported here rather than left for a referee to find. A momentum-resolved probe commutes with
lattice translation, so its Krylov space lies inside a single momentum block, $\Lambda_{\mathcal S}$
vanishes identically on that block and the certificate becomes non-vacuous at $48$--$54$ of
$300$ determinants and at $490$ of $3920$. The same measurement closes it: the block is $1/L$
of the sector against an exponential, reaching $0.89$ of the published fraction by $L=14$, and
three things about it are unmeasured (Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}).
The obstruction stands where the device measures, and the repair that looked
available to the proof is not one: measured rung by rung, the step that looked easiest to tighten
carries a total margin of a factor $1.05$ typically and $3.29$ at the worst point measured, which
moves the threshold by a fraction of one grid interval.

The two-sided bound is the instrument that locates that boundary. Let $\phi$ be the probe vector,
$A(\omega)$ the spectral function broadened to resolution $\eta$, $\mathcal S$ a set of determinants
and $A_{\mathcal S}$ the Rayleigh--Ritz reconstruction on its span. The $L_1$ error is bounded below
by the Born weight the subspace failed to capture, and above by that same weight together with the
Hamiltonian coupling that leaks across the boundary of the subspace, averaged over the resolution
window (Theorem~\ref{thm:leakage}, Sec.~\ref{sec:leakage}). The two terms of the upper bound are two
budgets, and only the first is bought by drawing more bitstrings. That is measured rather than
argued: on $\mathcal S^{\star}=\operatorname{supp}(\phi)$, the Born support of the probe
itself---exactly $(\tfrac12+\tfrac1L)$ of the sector, fixed by the probe and not by any ranking, so
that the captured weight is unity by construction with no tie-breaking and no zero-amplitude
padding---the first term vanishes identically while the relative $L_1$ error is still $0.43$ at
$L=6$ and $0.33$ at $L=8$ at $\eta=0.18\,t$, rising to $1.15$ and $0.90$ at $\eta=0.05\,t$, against a
hard ceiling of $2$. (These are the periodic rings; the open chain on which Sec.~\ref{sec:moments}
measures the same witness gives $0.28$ at $L=6$, and the two lattices are never pooled into one range
in this paper.) The error is indexed on the boundary of the subspace, in the basis in which the
device measures, and not on the weight the subspace captured; and that boundary, by the measurement
above, is everything.

Four bodies of work bound what is new here, and we state them before stating our own claims.
\emph{(i)~Concurrent and independent.} Patel, Rangi and Tam~\cite{patel2026} construct Green's
functions from sampled Krylov subspaces for dynamical mean-field theory~\cite{dmft1996}, at the same
system size
this work reaches, and cite it as ``a closely related sample-based approach to dynamical spectral
functions, constructed directly from bitstring-sampled subspaces.'' Their construction is
independent of ours; we make no priority claim against it, and none is needed.
\emph{(ii)~Classical dynamical DMRG arrived first.} Benthien and Jeckelmann~\cite{benthien2007}
computed the dynamical spin and charge structure factors and the one-particle spectral function of the
half-filled one-dimensional \emph{extended} Hubbard model in the Mott regime---three channels, on the
$V=0$ line the plain Hubbard model, and \emph{all three} are channels this paper reports: their spin
factor, our $S^{zz}(q,\omega)$, at $L=100$; their charge factor, our $S(q,\omega)$, at $L=120$; and
their $A(q,\omega)$, our $A(k,\omega)$, at $L=90$---nineteen years ago, and the
correction-vector literature treats the Hubbard chain at $L=48$ with $\eta=0.1\,t$, and
reaches $L=64$ in the Heisenberg chain and $48\times2$ sites in the $t$--$J$ ladder~\cite{nocera2016}. The sentence in the abstract of versions~1--2, that the
ground-state energy is where classical methods have caught up and dynamics is therefore the
defensible target, is \emph{inverted} in one dimension and is withdrawn: here, dynamics is where
the classical methods arrived first. \emph{(iii)~Hardware.} Frequency-resolved responses of
correlated lattice models have already been measured on devices far larger than the one used
here---a retarded spin Green's function of the one-dimensional Fermi--Hubbard model on an IBM
Heron processor at up to $30$ qubits~\cite{vilchez2025}; an angle-resolved-photoemission-like
$A(k,\omega)$ on a $27$-site chain at $54$ qubits~\cite{granet2026akw}; $S(q,\omega)$ of a
$20$-site Heisenberg chain benchmarked against a real copper-sulfate
crystal~\cite{quantinuum_dsf2026}; $S(q,\omega)$ on $50$ qubits against neutron scattering in
$\mathrm{KCuF_3}$~\cite{lee2026sqw}; and quench spectroscopy of $L=101$ spins with no ground-state
preparation at all~\cite{millar2026}. Our own device run is at $L=6$, and the subspace it recovers
spans its entire sector, so its agreement with exact diagonalization is exact by coverage rather
than a test of the method (Sec.~\ref{sec:honest}). \emph{(iv)~The invariant versus basis-dependent
dichotomy.} Tarabunga \emph{et al.}~\cite{tarabunga2026}, six weeks before version~1 of this work,
set out the same division on general grounds: occupation entropies are Gaussian invariants and
lower-bound the non-Gaussian gate count, while participation entropies in the natural-orbital
basis are basis dependent and upper-bound the classical simulation cost. Leone and
Bittel~\cite{leonebittel2026} gave a Gaussian monotone of the same class seventeen days before
version~1 of this work.
The priority for the dichotomy is theirs; what this work adds is the constructive instance those
papers do not contain, and the extension of that division from the determinant support to the
leakage term above (Sec.~\ref{sec:nogo}).

What versions~1 and~2 of this preprint published in place of Theorem~\ref{thm:leakage} was
different. They carried a weight-only error bound, Theorem~1(iii), and invoked it as the
certificate governing the reconstruction. It is false---by an analytic two-level counterexample,
which forces its constant to the trivial value
$2\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}$, and by counting, since from $L=8$ onward every operating point those
versions published lies below the combinatorial floor at which its second term has any content at
all. It fails wherever it was invoked: in $126$ of $126$ subspaces of the original sweep with
captured weight above $0.99$, and in $363$ of $363$ such subspaces of the pooled sweep behind
Fig.~\ref{fig:thm1iii-violation}. We retract it explicitly, together with the sentence that
repeated it in prose in the discussion, and replace it by Theorem~\ref{thm:leakage}: proved from
the resolvent identity alone, violated by none of the $606$ subspaces of that sweep, and stated
with the three hypotheses---common energy origin, normalization and support of the $L_1$ norm, and
exactness of the reference---that a user must check against the pipeline that produced
$A_{\mathcal S}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:retraction}).

A certificate that is never evaluated is a claim and not a result, and the retracted one was never
evaluated: neither of the two quantities it is written in is computed anywhere in the reproduction
repository. We evaluate the replacement on the subspaces this work actually used, at
$L=6,8,10,12,14$ (sector dimensions $300$ to $10\,306\,296$), and the outcome is the obstruction seen
from the operating point.
\emph{At every published fraction, at each of the five sizes evaluated, the leakage branch of the
bound is worse than the trivial bound $\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}(1+w_{\mathcal S})$}---vacuous by factors
of $1.66$ to $8.61$ over the twenty $(L,\eta)$ cells evaluated---and at $L=12$ that verdict is
converged in Krylov depth, so it cannot be moved by computing harder. The reconstructions reported in
this paper are therefore, at present, uncertified. Two things stand beside that sentence rather than
soften it. The certificate is calibrated: across all sixteen $(L,\eta)$ cells of the sweep its
crossing of the trivial bound occurs at a true relative $L_1$ error between $1.2\times10^{-3}$ and
$7.3\times10^{-3}$, median $2.8\times10^{-3}$, on a table whose true errors span five
decades---computed from $H$, $\mathcal S$, $\phi$ and $\eta$ alone, without the answer---with the
limit Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier} states in full: out of sample a two-parameter rule fitted in
$1-w_{\mathcal S}$ localizes the crossing about as tightly, and more tightly on ten of the fourteen
splits (a count moving between six and twelve as the constants are fitted), so we claim no
superiority for the certificate as a tracker of the error against the captured weight. And the
reconstruction itself is untouched: an independent implementation, with different code,
normalization and reference, recovers the published acceptance fractions at the four sizes it
covers, the largest discrepancy being $7\%$---at $L=12$, against the coarse replication-grid value
$0.18$, and under $2\%$ against the $0.17$ the scan itself reports. What the measurement retires is
the word \emph{certified} applied to those fractions, not the fractions themselves.

The obstruction has a second face, and the second half of this paper measures it: if the certificate
cannot be made non-vacuous where sampling operates, can it at least be replaced by an a priori
predictor, computed from the state before any circuit runs? The natural candidates are the efficiently measurable monotones of
fermionic non-Gaussianity, and on number-conserving states the one-body member of that family
collapses to a single one-particle-density-matrix invariant,
$\mathcal F_1=4\,\mathrm{tr}[\gamma(1-\gamma)]$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse}), equal to twice the
effectively-unpaired-electron index for spin-restricted natural orbitals and not for the
spin-polarized members of the suite. An
invariant of the occupation spectrum cannot see a boundary. Along a geminal orbit every order of
it is fixed while the determinant support runs from $O(K)$ to $2^{K}$ at bond dimension $2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}); and on fibers where every invariant \emph{and} the captured weight
are frozen to $10^{-14}$, the subspace-aware certificate still separates a non-vacuous value from
the trivial bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:leakinv}). The statement is one of non-determination and
non-vacuity, not of non-existence: an invariant upper bound on the leakage does exist, and it is
the trivial one. The same discipline applies to our own statistics, which were reported the wrong
way up. Analysed within the six $(L,N)$ strata it contains, the Hubbard interaction sweep gives a
rank correlation of $\mathcal F_1$ with the determinant support of exactly $-1$ in all six:
stratified Kendall $\tau=-1.000$, zero concordant pairs out of $60$, exact one-sided permutation
$p=3.3\times10^{-13}$. That is not an absence of relation but a deterministic monotone one with
the opposite sign, whose sign is not stable from family to family---exactly what an orbital
invariant compared against a basis-dependent count must do. Both pooled readings of versions~1--2
are withdrawn in Sec.~\ref{sec:nogo:chi}; one of the two was in our own favour.

\emph{We claim no quantum advantage in this paper.} No result reported here would support one; the
classical-simulability boundary is charted rather than contested (Sec.~\ref{sec:honest}); and the
one quantitative statement this work makes about its own cost---that the basis-dependent bond
dimension empirically tracks the determinant support---argues, wherever that bond dimension is
small, for a tensor network rather than for a sampler.

The paper is organized in that order. Section~\ref{sec:method} fixes the primitive and states,
channel by channel, which reconstructions carry a declared shot budget and which are exact Haydock
references. Section~\ref{sec:leakage} proves the bound and retracts its predecessor;
Sec.~\ref{sec:moments} isolates what the subspace structure does certify---moment exactness through
order $2K+1$ and no further, with the proof the code actually realizes (Theorem~\ref{thm:moments})
and a non-circular shot count replacing the published one (Proposition~\ref{prop:shots}).
Section~\ref{sec:frontier} evaluates the certificate, measures the obstruction that places its
threshold and calibrates the crossing that survives it; Sec.~\ref{sec:nogo} closes the same door
from the a priori side. Sections~\ref{sec:prices} and~\ref{sec:physics} price support, resolution
and shots and validate the primitive against the opposite finite-size behaviour of the charge and
spin scales of the Hubbard chain (Table~\ref{tab:gaps}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gapscaling});
Sec.~\ref{sec:honest} reports the device runs, the scope of the molecular suite, and the region in
which this work's own thesis argues against a quantum advantage. The systems throughout are the
half-filled one-dimensional Hubbard model at $U/t=8$---rings and open chains at $L=4$ to $14$, by
exact diagonalization and matrix-free Lanczos---and a suite of nineteen molecular active spaces.
Three different counts share the symbol $\mathcal S$ in this literature and are kept apart here: the
static ground-state support of Sec.~\ref{sec:nogo}, the dynamical support of Sec.~\ref{sec:prices},
and the set of configurations the sampler actually returned, which is the $\mathcal S$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:leakage}.
