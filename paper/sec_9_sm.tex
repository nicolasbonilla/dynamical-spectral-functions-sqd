\section{Noise sweeps, the molecular suite and classical reach}
\label{sm:noisemol}

\subsection{The noise sweeps and the molecular suite}
\label{sec:scope:hw}

The noise results of this work are controlled classical simulations: under a synthetic per-qubit
bit-flip channel, recovery is compared with no recovery on the same corrupted bitstrings, and the gain
grows with the noise (Figs.~\ref{fig:noise} and~\ref{fig:noiserec}). Where the recovered error falls
below the noiseless value, that comparison is not used here: it is decided by the size of the
recovered subspace, which the sweeps do not record (Sec.~\ref{sm:scope}). A study under a simple
noise model reports the mechanism~\cite{vaquero2026}. That study also finds that recovery from
randomly generated configurations alone builds accurate CI spaces, and no such control was run here.

\paragraph{The molecular suite.} On the seven Hamiltonians of the nineteen-molecule suite (Sec.~\ref{sec:scope:mol}, Table~\ref{tab:molscope}) whose sampled subspaces do not contain the probe's Krylov space (sectors of dimension $20$ to $300$), the sampling primitive of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} reproduces the orbital-projected $A(\omega)$ to a relative $L_1$ between $2.2\times10^{-6}$ and $1.0\times10^{-4}$ from $21\%$ to $50\%$ of the sector, a consistency check of the primitive outside the Hubbard family and not a scaling statement. The other twelve reconstructions are exact by construction, because their sampled subspaces, of at most twelve determinants, already contain the probe's Krylov space, so the suite cannot show that the reconstruction is efficient across chemistry.

\subsection{What the cost tracker implies for classical simulation}
\label{sec:scope:boomerang}

The cost tracker of Sec.~\ref{sec:nogo}, the bond dimension $\chi$, is by definition the cost
parameter of a matrix-product representation of the same state: wherever it says the determinant
support is small, a matrix-product state is small too. A sampler can therefore be needed only for a
target whose minimal bond dimension is large in every ordering while the sampled support stays
compact, a property of geometry and orbital connectivity that two-dimensional lattices and molecular
orbital graphs would test and that nothing here measures (Sec.~\ref{sm:scope}). Every target measured
here---Hubbard rings at $L\le14$, nineteen active spaces, and a largest minimal bond dimension of $42$
over the $74$ deposited exact ground states---lies on the other side: there is no computation
performed here that a classical method cannot perform, and we make no advantage claim at any size
reached. The literature shows the same pattern. The $7\,260$ trajectories of a $120$-qubit
Fermi--Hubbard dynamics experiment~\cite{qctrlfh2026} were reproduced by tensor contraction at bond
dimension $32$~\cite{ouyang2026}, the pattern in which tensor-network and Pauli-propagation methods
have overtaken earlier hardware demonstrations~\cite{kim2023,tindall2024,begusic2024}, with Pauli
propagation since extended to real-time correlators~\cite{kemper2026};

the energy of any single-layer unitary cluster-Jastrow circuit, the largest ($77$-qubit) experiment of
Ref.~\cite{robledomoreno2025} included, is now computed classically in polynomial
time~\cite{belagali2026}, and an extended matchgate simulator evaluates, to additive approximation,
the Born-rule probabilities of hardware-sampled cluster-Jastrow bitstrings at a cost exponential only in
the magnitudes of the controlled-phase angles~\cite{extraferm}; on the ground-state energy the strong classical selectors already win on
cost~\cite{reinholdt2025,zhangotten2026}, and even optimally ordered SQD subspaces need a number of
configurations that grows exponentially with lattice size for Heisenberg and Hubbard ground
states~\cite{gaberle2026}.

\paragraph{A dequantization test.} On an emulated, under-sampled head for $\mathrm{N_2}$ ($R=2.5$~\AA; $1\,000$ shots drawn from the exact FCI marginal and corrupted by a synthetic Heron-class noise model, ten paired seeds; no device data), a classical Epstein--Nesbet prior~\cite{cipsi1973} applied to the same histogram cuts the energy error by $36\%$, from $38.22$ to $24.38$~mHa (paired $t_9=12.37$, all ten seeds), while a generative flow network~\cite{bengio2021} fused onto that prior moves it a further $1.80$~mHa, a difference ten paired seeds do not resolve ($t_9=1.19$) and whose sign reverses between two five-seed halves; at $500$ shots the fused generator is worse than the classical control (Sec.~\ref{app:dequant}). On this instance the dominant determinants are supplied by the sampled histogram (emulated, so the test does not measure whether a device state would supply them) and completed by a classical prior rather than manufactured by a learned generator, and the largest improvement available to an under-sampled head costs no quantum resources at all.
