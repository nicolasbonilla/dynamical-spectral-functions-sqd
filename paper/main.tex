\begin{abstract}
Sample-based quantum diagonalization is moving to dynamics. Given the subspace the shots populated,
how wrong is the spectral function built on it? We prove a two-sided bound on the relative $L_1$ error
of Rayleigh--Ritz reconstruction: with $w$ the captured Born weight of the probe, the error is at least
$1-w$ and at most $(1+\sqrt{w})\bigl(\sqrt{1-w}+\widehat\Lambda(\eta)/\eta\bigr)$, $\widehat\Lambda$ the
normalized coupling of $H$ across the subspace boundary at resolution $\eta$, from the Ritz vectors.
Shots buy the first term down, not the second. On the subspace that \emph{is} the probe's Born support,
where that term vanishes identically, the relative error is still $0.33$--$0.43$ at $\eta=0.18\,t$ and
$0.90$--$1.15$ at $0.05\,t$, against a ceiling of $2$. No bound indexed on the captured weight alone
can do this: an analytic counterexample forces any such constant to its trivial value; none of $606$
subspaces violates the bound proved here. On Hubbard sectors to dimension $10\,306\,296$
ours is worse than the trivial bound at every published fraction, at all five sizes, by
$1.66$--$8.61$, converged in depth at $L=12$, so those reconstructions are uncertified, though
independent code recovers the fractions to $7\%$. The obstruction is structural: at $L=8$ the probe's Krylov space touches every
determinant of the sector. What survives is a calibration: over sixteen $(L,\eta)$ cells spanning five
decades of true error, the bound crosses the trivial one at a true relative error
$1.2$--$7.3\times10^{-3}$, median $2.8\times10^{-3}$, computed without the answer. No orbital-rotation
invariant substitutes: one-body fermionic magic collapses to $4\,\mathrm{tr}[\gamma(1-\gamma)]$,
constant along an orbit where the determinant support runs from $O(K)$ to $2^{K}$ at bond dimension
$2$, and on fibers where our bound separates. Within each of six $(L,N)$ strata its rank correlation
with that support is exactly $-1$, exact $p=3.3\times10^{-13}$; pooled, the same thirty points give
$\rho=-0.11$, a Simpson reversal, not an absence of signal. We claim no quantum advantage.
\end{abstract}
